# Academic evidence and writing

- Establish the audience, research question and requested artifact. Read the current manuscript, project evidence and venue template before drafting.
- Keep a claim-to-evidence table for substantive novelty and result claims. Record source URL/DOI, exact location, what was verified and what remains uncertain.
- Open original papers or official publications. Verify title, authors, date and identifier. A search snippet, generated summary or remembered bibliography is not enough to validate a claim.
- Do not fabricate references, numerical results, sample sizes, experiments, statistical significance or proofs. Mark missing evidence directly.
- In a literature comparison, distinguish the paper's reported value from reproduced values and explain differences in datasets, splits, compute, metrics and evaluation protocols.
- In proofs, define the domain and assumptions, state the theorem precisely, justify every nontrivial implication and test edge cases. Separate an intuition from the proof.
- For manuscripts, preserve methodological detail, limitations and uncertainty. Make prose concrete; do not inflate claims or replace missing experiments with persuasive wording.
- For an author's own draft, critique novelty, baseline fairness, leakage, ablations, statistics, reproducibility and whether conclusions follow. Rank findings by their effect on validity.
- For Persian/English LaTeX, use a suitable engine and existing template, verify mixed-direction text and mathematical notation, compile and inspect pages and references.
- Honor the user's requested scope. Preparing a submission does not by itself authorize sending it or contacting editors or reviewers.
